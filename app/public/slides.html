The story of Takeback

Refunds are the awkward moment in every crypto shop. The shop asks the customer for an address, types in an amount and hopes nobody made a mistake.

Crypto.com's own help page says it "cannot send the refund amount directly back to the payment wallet address".

So we put the refund inside the payment. You pay, and the same transaction hands you a receipt that can only ever pay back what you paid.

Source: help.crypto.com, "How to get a refund" 2 / 6

Benefits of Takeback

  • Refund in one tap. Nobody types an address.
  • Exactly the amount paid, once, to whoever holds the receipt.
  • Partial refunds keep the rest owed on a new receipt.
  • A stolen server key can't drain the shop: the contract limits what it can sign.
  • The shop's books rebuild from the chain alone.
  • A refund lands in seconds: 1.8 to 4.3 seconds from the click in our 4 timed mainnet runs. A card refund reaches the customer "approximately 5-10 business days later" (Stripe's refund docs).

BCH usage

  • Live on Bitcoin Cash mainnet. The receipt is a CashTokens NFT, minted by a CashScript covenant inside the payment itself.
  • Built on the May 2026 upgrade: the covenant's loops check every other input and output.
  • Phones connect with WizardConnect. tokenexplorer.cash independently checks the receipt's metadata against the chain.

The vision for Takeback

  • Who pays: shops that take BCH, and the processors and point-of-sale wallets that serve them. Refunds are their weak spot today.
  • Why now: CoinGate's checkout takes BCH again since 12 October 2026, PUSD gives BCH a dollar token, and WizardConnect lets any web checkout ask a phone wallet to sign.
  • How it earns: a small fee per receipt, or a licence for processors. (A plan: no price tested yet.)
  • Next: PUSD-priced receipts, a pay button any shop can add, and a Refund button inside wallets for any Takeback receipt.
